Marvin had gone to a party in San Clemente with some old friends. It was an impactful enough experience that he told me about it while walking over to the parking lot showers after finishing a surf, on whichever following weekday we went out. He described mingling with creative types, the party's premise being a screening of some friend-of-friend's surf documentary. Apparently, they rip, and presumably cut together footage of ripping overhead Jell-O barrels in Bali or something. Or so I imagine. Writing this now, I'm chuckling a bit at the light disdain, and frankly, jealousy, with which I've described this event and film and allude to the personalities of people I've never met. I trust Marvin to be a good judge of character; I'm sure his San Clemente friends are nice people. 

I think this disdain comes from the fact that I don't rip, at least not yet. I've been properly working at getting better at surfing for about 2 years. Marvin was (and is) a key accountability buddy. Without his coaxing, which typically takes the form of an evening phone call in which he "wishes to discuss the surf" for the following morning, I would probably have 10% the reps that I do now. And yet, I'm still not where I want to be. Never have I spent so much time and effort working for a thing with so little to show for it. 

I digress. The disdain. Disdain is a strong feeling, and at that moment it provided sufficient passion for me to ask "why can't *we* have a surf documentary?"

## Surf Documentary Ideas
- Shoot on 2000s era DSLR that has footage quality that looks like my Canon EOS Rebel XTi. Want vintage look which is cool but not too vintage to be pretentious
- Write and record original soundtrack
- Call it "Oyster Day" in reference to Marvin's saying "the day is your oyster" after surfing
  - Subtitle: a surf video (homage to stickers)
- Interview Bolsa Dave
- Side plot to figure out who/what is "surf video" stickers around Long Beach
- Does this need to have a throughline? Ideas:
  - No. Surf clips, surf video investigation, complete hodge podge. Several vignettes. Meta through line
  - Yes. Track improvement progress over some time span and reflect on what surfing means to people
  - Yes. Focus on Bolsa Chica and do profiles on the locals
  - Yes. Focus on a strike mission trip to a place with adventure
  - Yes. Focus on surfboard shaping
- Plot point: Meet, surf with, and film Marvin's friend of friend who inspired this
- Build a microphone to record music voiceovers talking heads etc?

## Surf Video
Another inspiration for this project has been these "SURF VIDEO" stickers I see all over Long Beach, near where I live. Bold stickers. Super generic though, literally impossible to find any info online because Google thinks I just want to watch surf videos. I took a Sunday afternoon to take photographs of every one that I could find, and interviewed people to try and determine their origin. Every sticker I encounter is a call to action for me to [fight the resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_War_of_Art_(book)) and make this surf video. Here are a selection of my better photos and an account of my leads so far:

**Ozzy, my barber** had no idea who they are, generally amused by the sticker being put on his barbershop door.

**Skate shop employee at 4th street skate with long hair and stubble** had no idea who they are but acknowledged seeing them everywhere

**Boutique employees at Seams on 4th street who sell surfboards but don't surf** believe it's a band, whose former drummer (not on Instagram unfortunately) works at the military surplus store on Bellflower.

**Employee at military surplus store on Bellflower who answered the phone** is not the drummer for Surf Video, and has no clue which employee I could be talking about.

**ACLU donation jockey outside the brewery on 4th st** moved down here from Oakland, where they knew the tagging scene and are convinced that the stickers are a cost-saving collaboration between two taggers/graffiti artists ostensibly named Surf and Video.

**Bartender at Vine on 4th st** knows nothing, is amused, and acknowledges seeing them everywhere while grabbing a hoppy refresher and a non-alcoholic IPA from the glass door fridge behind the bar. Request for a glass for the beverages is affirmed, and ultimately forgotten in the rush. I was mildly annoyed at myself for not having the courage to remind the bartender of this.

**Izzy Romero** has seen these stickers everywhere, and offers to ask his tagger friend in the scene for more information. I gotta remember to ask him about this again on Monday.
